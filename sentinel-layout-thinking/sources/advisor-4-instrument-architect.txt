## Solid defense of the existing layout

Not nostalgia — the current frame is load-bearing for real reasons:

1. **Command picture is a product principle, not a style** (`PRODUCT.md`): map + evidence + TALON + one dominant action *together*. Any layout that makes you hunt for one of those four breaks the product thesis.
2. **Orb dead-center bottom is doing ergonomics work.** Authorization sits on the centerline where both hands/eyes converge, equidistant from both rails — neutral territory between “world” (map) and “interpretation” (rails). Moving AI *and* gates to one side makes that side the “AI side of the screen,” which subtly biases you toward TALON’s recommendation when the human is supposed to be the decider.
3. **Dual rails encode a real split:** left = *what is* (observation), right = *what it means* (synthesis). That’s not decoration — it’s the AI-association story made spatial. The archive docs and vault `06` both call this macro layout **authoritative**.
4. **Top status strip matches how ops rooms and ATC actually scan:** alerts/phase/clock get upper-field fixation. Eye-tracking and FAA-style guidance both put “always monitor” info up top, not in a bottom periphery.
5. **Bottom is already the commit zone** — ActionBar owns gates. Research (SAP Fiori, touch tables) agrees: *workflow primaries* belong at the bottom. You already have that half right.
6. **Stability is a safety feature.** “Command bar remains physically stable,” orb “never moves” — in a 90-second emergency flow, muscle memory is infrastructure. Layout that rearranges under you is a bug, not a feature.

**Verdict:** the skeleton is defensible and domain-plausible. Your instincts aren’t “wrong,” but they mostly want to **rebalance roles**, not necessarily bulldoze the frame.

## Where the current layout is legitimately weak

- **TALON is split-brained:** orb/voice bottom + synthesis right + chips historically in the action bar. Three homes = weak “one agent, one surface” identity. Your instinct to give TALON *a better home* is correct — the question is which home, not whether.
- **Right rail can feel like a passive log** rather than a control surface for the agent. If future TALON is meant to *drive* information across rails, the right rail is the natural “agent desk,” but today it’s more “output tape.”

- **Rails are static tab stacks** — fine for dense expert use, but they don’t express “the situation just changed.” Your “panels light up when TALON updates them” idea targets a real gap: *attention management*.

## Your ideas, pressure-tested

### 1. “Move TALON to the right, proper chat + buttons”

**What’s right about it:** External pattern research *also* puts copilots on the right (M365 Copilot, ATC strip-bay-on-right). Your right rail is *already* TALON’s synthesis zone — consolidating presence there kills the split-brain. Chat-shaped transcript + explicit command buttons is how operators expect an agent to look in 2026.

**What it costs:**
- The **orb dies or becomes a chip**. Vault `09` and DESIGN.md treat the orb as fixed identity, 56px legibility test, blooms as the authorization geometry. That’s a full identity redesign, not a panel move.
- **Authorization migrates** from center-neutral to right-side. Biases toward the AI side of the screen.
- Map loses nothing horizontally… but **bottom opens up**, which is either a gift (bigger map, cleaner edge) or a hole (where does the *one dominant action* live?).
- Where does the old right rail go? If TALON swallows it, fine. If you “move the right rail somewhere,” you’ve just invented a third region problem.

**Honest take:** Moving TALON’s *conversation body* right is the strongest single move in your list. Moving *authorization* right is the part I’d fight. A hybrid (below) gets you most of the feeling without torching the gate geometry.

### 2. “Top nav → bottom nav, TALON curved into navbar center, top left empty”

This is the boldest idea, and it’s **half right by industry standards, half wrong by scan science**.

**Half right:**
- Bottom bar as a **redesigned command deck** (status + composer + primary commit) matches thumb zones, desk-console foot rails, and Fiori’s “footer = workflow primary.”
- A **notch/curve cutout for TALON** (think console HUD, or a home-indicator cradle) would give the orb a *built-in berth* — actually a nicer physical metaphor than orb-floating-over-map.
- Empty top = more map + fewer competing fixations *if* nothing critical moves down incorrectly.

**Half wrong:**
- **Alerts, phase, clock belong high.** Move `Intelligence ↔ Operations`, incident ID, T+ clock, exception count to the bottom and you’re asking the operator to scan the *least* natural zone for “is everything still true?” Eye-tracking on dashboards punishes this.
- Bottom nav capacity is small; your top bar currently carries a lot. You’d be forced into overflow or a second tier — which recreates the top bar with extra steps.
- Empty top fights “one command picture” optics: embeds and screenshots read as “app missing chrome” unless something else owns identity/mode.

**Discussion verdict:** Don’t *flip* top→bottom. **Split the roles:**
- **Top stays a thin truth strip** (incident, phase, clock, alarm count, TALON state chip) — maybe *thinner* and less button-y.
- **Bottom becomes the real deck**: redesigned nav segments + TALON cradle/curve + dominant action — i.e. ActionBar and Navbar *merge downward* into one purposeful instrument edge, top gets quieter, not empty.

That’s your idea with the evidence problem removed.

### 3. “Stacked panel windows + border slider scrub + TALON pops panels”

You’re describing a **scrub stack** (headers always visible, edge handle, flick between layers) plus **AI-driven attention popouts**.

**The scrub stack — when it works:** great for *mutually exclusive* tools inside *one* rail (VS Code activity bar, QGC’s accordion right panel). Fast, tactile, feels futuristic, loves touch.

**Where it fails for *this* product:** your command picture requires **evidence and TALON co-visible** with the map. If left and right both become single scrubbable stacks, you can’t have “zone evidence *and* confidence *and* session” up at once — you’ve traded density for motion. Logic/Final Cut inspectors on small monitors get the same complaint: hiding context to show context.

**Middle path that keeps your idea alive:**
- **Left rail = scrub stack** (observation is naturally one-thing-at-a-time: zones / assets / evidence / stream). Headers as a vertical or edge slider — good.
- **Right rail = pinned TALON** with *internal* stack for session/sources/exceptions — agent never fully disappears (research: Figma’s left-side AI *replacing* navigation was a disaster; never let the agent be a stack layer you can scrub away).
- **Slider on the border** is a nice touch primitive — but on desktop it needs a click/keyboard equivalent; on touch, 44px+ hit areas; and scrubbing must never be the *only* way to reach a human gate (gates stay unstacked, always visible).

**TALON “pops panels into active state”:** this is the right *idea* with a dangerous *implementation*.
- ✅ **Attention, not rearrangement:** panel edge lights, header gets a state chip, unread badge, soft focus ring — “TALON wants eyes here.”
- ⚠️ **Actually opening/swapping panels for you** breaks spatial memory and the “panel state and spatial state agree” principle if the operator didn’t cause it.
- Best pattern: TALON **proposes focus** (“Evidence · new FLIR frame”) → one flash/expand → operator click or TALON-open with a visible “opened by TALON · undo” affordance. AI arranges *attention*; operator keeps *layout sovereignty*. That’s also a better portfolio story about human-AI boundaries than “AI drags your windows around.”

---

### 4. “TALON backend interconnects all rails; TALON controls information”

As a **product north star**: strong — it’s an information-orchestration agent, not a chatbot glued to a dashboard.

As a **literal UI behavior** (agent freely rewrites rails): risky in a system whose golden rules are human authorization and honest state. If TALON silently reflows evidence, the operator can’t trust that what they’re looking at is what *they* last verified.

The mature version: TALON owns a **priority/attention layer** over shared panels (ordering, badges, recommended focus, deprioritizing noise in Operations density modes — you already have density modes that do a *scripted* version of this). Rails stay structurally stable; **content priority becomes agent-driven**. That’s “TALON controls information” without “TALON controls the furniture.”

### 5. Desk console vs handheld tablet

These are **two products sharing one brain**, not one skin:

| | Desk-embedded console | Handheld tablet |
|---|---|---|
| Scan | Top strip + center map (high fixation) | Bottom thumb deck; map above |
| Input | Hover OK, dense labels, mouse precision | No hover, 44–48px targets, gestures |
| Rails | Dual frost rails fine | Rails want to become **sheets/drawers** |
| TALON | Right pane + bottom cradle works | **Bottom sheet + composer** wins |
| Layout stability | Fixed viewport — muscle memory safe | Orientation/safe-area — needs detents |

QGroundControl literally ships **different computer vs tablet UIs**. Wildfire EOC tools do too. Pretending one absolute layout serves a desk embed *and* a handheld is how you get a compromised average.

**Implication for your direction:** design the **desk frame** as canonical (matches 1920×1080 acceptance target). Treat tablet as a **recomposition rule**: same regions, bottom-weighted TALON, rails → sheets, scrub stacks shine *there* more than on desk. Your slider-stack idea is *more* tablet-native than desktop-native — which is a point in its favor if you’re serious about the handheld story.

## How I’d frame the tension (still just talk)

You’re pulled between two valid archetypes:

1. **The instrument** (current): fixed zones, stable orb, dual rails, calm density. Defensible, on-thesis, slightly static, TALON identity a bit diluted across three places.
2. **The adaptive console** (your sketches): bottom deck with TALON cradle, stacked/scrubbable rails, agent-driven attention. More “extreme futuristic,” more product-demo energy, more risk to gates, scan order, and muscle memory.

The portfolio-winning move isn’t picking team “keep” or team “rebuild.” It’s **keeping the four-surface contract and the human gate geometry** while letting you redesign *within* each surface:

- Quieter top truth strip (not empty, not flipped)
- Bottom deck = nav segments + **TALON curve/cradle** + dominant action (your curve idea, applied to ActionBar’s world)
- TALON conversation **body** gains a real right-side home (your right-move) while the **cradle/commit** stays center — presence and memory reunified as one system with two anchors, or fully right if you’re willing to kill the orb’s centrality
- Left rail becomes the **scrub stack** (your slider) — observation is allowed to be one-at-a-time
- Right rail stays **pinned agent**, internal stack only
- “TALON updates” = **attention signals + optional suggested focus**, never silent furniture rearrangement
- Tablet = recomposition mode where your bottom-deck and scrub ideas become primary
