§01 · Business Context

Wildfire response fails at the interface, not the hardware.

48 min
Average time from anomaly detection to first authorized action — spent manually assembling context across three disconnected systems.
90 sec
Target: detection to verified authority escalation via the AUD-90-0847 packet. Containment planning follows after the notification is sent.
3
Human decision gates in the entire Intelligence phase. Everything else is TALON-governed — with full audit trail.
The Problem
Sector leads must manually cross-reference thermal sensors, satellite IR, and municipal burn-permit records to verify a single anomaly. The drone fleet is ready to deploy within seconds. The operator is still building a picture. That assembly cost — not hardware latency — is the 48 minutes.
Validation
Design simulation against documented emergency dispatch protocols. The 48-minute baseline represents the manual information assembly time. The 90-second target is simulation-validated — not field-measured. Section 7 addresses this gap directly.
§02 · System Architecture

Two phases. One persistent map. Seven agents handling everything the operator shouldn't have to.

The interface collapses four procedural stages into two cognitive modes — with a hard transition at authority notification. The layout stays constant. The information density adjusts automatically.

Phase A · Intelligence
Is this real, dangerous, and authority-notifiable?

0–90 seconds. Operator assesses anomalies, dispatches verification scouts, resolves any permit conflicts, and sends the escalation packet. Interface: verbose, rich context, every evidence source visible.

Phase B · Operations
Coordinate resources. Evacuate. Supervise containment.

Post-notification. Drone missions executing, evacuation active, biometric feeds live. Interface: compact single-line telemetry only — no prose, exception-only alerts.

§04 · Design Decisions

Five decisions that defined the system. Each one was a real trade-off.

D-01
Why the 90 seconds ends at escalation, not containment
V1 claimed full containment in 90 seconds — including staging, evacuation, and residential clearance. No incident commander would authorize tactical deployment before basic verification.
Core Pivot
A
Keep "containment in 90 seconds"
Rejected — operationally impossible before incident verification
B
Full AI autonomy, remove the human gate
Rejected — illegal for airspace operations over occupied residential areas
C
90 seconds ends at verified authority escalation
AUD-90-0847 packet dispatched. Containment staging begins in Phase B.
Trade-off
Lost the flashier demo metric. Gained a claim that survives a conversation with a real emergency dispatcher.
Result: The 90-second timer stops when Asha clicks "Notify Authorities." No artificial pressure on the operations that follow.
D-02
The mandatory 6-second friction — designing against automation bias
Under time pressure, operators rubber-stamp AI recommendations to reduce cognitive load. In wildfire dispatch, that produces false-positive drone deployments over occupied residential areas at 3 AM.
Failure Handling
A
Silent background resolution
Rejected — sensor lag creates a window where the AI resolves the wrong state without Asha knowing
B
Full modal block until satellite IR resolves
Rejected — blocking the map canvas during a size-up is a design failure of its own kind
C
High-contrast warning + mandatory checkbox — the Forced Calibrator
Locks primary CTAs, requires explicit permit verification. Map canvas stays fully visible throughout.
Trade-off
4–6 seconds of mandatory friction on every permit conflict. Accepted because a false-positive drone deployment over residential housing is the failure this friction prevents.
Result: The Forced Calibrator is the only intentional speed penalty in the system. Every other interaction minimizes latency — this one deliberately imposes it.
D-03
Evidence matrix over confidence score — giving operators something to argue with
TALON surfaced a single percentage: "87% ignition probability." Operators could accept or reject — but had nothing concrete to evaluate, interrogate, or push back on.
AI Trust
A
Single confidence percentage
Rejected — a score that can't be interrogated collapses the operator into a rubber stamp
B
Score + paragraph explanation
Rejected — prose doesn't map to fast yes/no evaluation under a 90-second constraint
C
Evidence matrix — individual chips per source
Thermal delta, LiDAR, satellite IR, burn permit status — each labeled, each showing its own confirmation state.
Trade-off
Costs more screen real estate than a percentage. Accepted because operators who understand which signal is uncertain make better overrides — and correct overrides improve the AI over time.
Result: If TALON is wrong, Asha knows exactly which sensor to scrutinize — not just that "the AI seems less confident."
D-04
LiDAR vectors over HD video — designing for real infrastructure
Remote wildfire perimeters run on degraded cell, not fiber. At 340ms latency, HD video streams saturate the bandwidth. Standard interfaces freeze behind loading spinners.
Infrastructure
A
Loading rings — wait for the feed to buffer
Rejected — a frozen interface during active rescue is operationally worse than no interface
B
Radio-only fallback — disable all telemetry
Rejected — complete loss of spatial awareness; falls back to 1970s emergency response capability
C
LiDAR vector mode — drop HD, keep coordinates
Satellite opacity → 0.04. HD video dropped. Lightweight vector-drawn spatial paths prioritized.
Trade-off
Operator loses raw video feeds. Accepted because spatial coordinate accuracy under degraded cell is more operationally valuable than high-fidelity video arriving 30 seconds late.
Result: Under simulated 340ms degradation, full spatial awareness retained. Zero interface lockup. Designed for Bharat-scale connectivity reality.
D-05
The interface simplifies as the emergency peaks
The same information density across all four phases — including during peak Operations when the operator's cognitive capacity is most constrained.
Cognitive Load
A
Separate screen layout per phase
Rejected — forces spatial reorientation at exactly the wrong moment
B
User-controlled density toggle
Rejected — adds a preference decision during active emergency coordination
C
Automatic density scaling on phase transition
Same layout grid. Verbose cards in Phase A → compact single-line strips in Phase B. No reorientation.
Trade-off
Removes operator agency over their information view. Accepted because cognitive capacity shrinks under load — one fewer decision is a feature, not a limitation.
Result: Same grid. Different density. The operator's spatial model never resets across the entire incident lifecycle.

§08 · Outcomes & Reflection

What I'd measure. And what I'd change.

Core Validation
Time to first decision

From alert to dispatch action. Target: under 15 seconds. Failure mode: operators re-reading the brief because it doesn't surface the right information first.

Safety Signal
Dismissed alert accuracy

What percentage of dismissed alerts became real incidents? Anything above 2% triggers immediate system review. The goal is accurate dismissals, not zero.

Trust Calibration
Operator override rate

Target: 15–40%. Too low suggests rubber-stamping. Too high suggests TALON's plans aren't worth acting on. Both are system failures.

Governance Check
Calibrator false-positive rate

How often does the Forced Calibrator fire on a genuine fire? Measures permit database coverage quality. Friction should be proportional to real conflict probability.

What still needs field validation
The 90-second target is a design simulation estimate — not measured data from trained dispatchers under real conditions. Whether the Forced Calibrator's friction is acceptable under actual emergency pressure requires testing I haven't done. TALON agent latency under real sensor disagreements is also untested.
If I rebuilt this tomorrow
Less time on the Operations phase density transitions, more time testing the evidence matrix with actual operators — specifically whether chip-based sensor presentation maps to how dispatchers actually think, or whether the mental model doesn't match. Voice correction also needed more iterations than it got.
If the system can secretly override Asha,
Asha is the illusion of control.
